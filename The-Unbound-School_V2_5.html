Step 02 / Comparative Analysis

IGCSE vs CBSE vs ICSE

An objective, educational overview to help families understand how structural subject flexibility, practical commitments, and exam windows shape a student's high-school journey.

Dimension Cambridge IGCSE CBSE ICSE
Subject Structure Linear framework with focused core disciplines and targeted electives. Standardized national curriculum framework across central boards. Comprehensive multi-subject curriculum across broad groupings.
Subject Flexibility High flexibility; electives tailored to candidate aptitude and target streams. Prescribed subject bundles within fixed traditional streams. Pre-configured subject packages with mandatory group boundaries.
Practical Component Central to learning; experimental design, measurement error, and lab assessment. Practical evaluations conducted per internal school schemes and board criteria. Extensive lab record coursework and internal project assessments.
Examination Window November Series (enables a 6-month head start into 11th). February – March Annual Board Examinations. February – March Annual Board Examinations.
Curriculum Approach Inquiry-led discovery, scientific evidence analysis, and conceptual mastery. Syllabus-driven breadth with high alignment to national entrance tests. Exhaustive factual recall, strong language emphasis, and detailed coursework.
Parent Takeaway IGCSE provides greater flexibility, stronger practical orientation and an earlier Grade 10 examination window, creating a useful head start into Grade 11.

Senior Secondary Continuum

Highlights of Cambridge A/AS Level

Cambridge A/AS Level serves as Your Primary Doorway to Study Abroad, providing the depth, independent inquiry, and subject mastery demanded by leading international universities.

  • • Cambridge A/AS Level represents the senior secondary stage of Cambridge international education.
  • • Board examinations are officially scheduled in November, February, and June.
  • • The Unbound School prioritises the November and February windows where appropriate to prevent learning interruptions.
  • • Language Rigor: English is assessed only once across the combined Grade 11–12 period.
